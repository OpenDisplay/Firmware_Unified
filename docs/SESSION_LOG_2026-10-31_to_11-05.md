## Arc A — Spectra 6 7.3″: closed negative (Oct 31 22:31 → Nov 1 16:30)

Transcripts `9f5499d9`, `81b990e5`. **→ `[[project_spectra6_custom_lut_closed]]`,
`[[project_spectra6_prior_work_read_findings_first]]`, `[[project_spectra6_measurement_gotchas]]`**

The original goal: more than 6 colours on the `en05_ep397yr_uart` 7.3″ Spectra 6 panel
(**SPD1657A rev 0x04**). Ran five user-supplied round plans plus a two-hour final attempt.

**Verdict: custom/register LUTs are impossible on this silicon. Do not re-attempt.** Closed with a
controlled **2×2** (`LUT_SEL` × bank-filled), not a fuzz:

| `LUT_SEL` | banks `0x20`–`0x29` | busy_ms |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | untouched | 22332 (normal) |
| 0 | filled | 26012 (hung) |
| 1 | untouched | **22336 (normal)** |
| 1 | filled | 26010 (hung) |

`LUT_SEL=1` alone gives a stock refresh → **the bit is inert** (fused off). The hang tracks the
*bank writes*, which stall the controller regardless. Second tell: doubling every `TP` didn't
change duration at all (60002 vs 60004 ms) — a real LUT cannot ignore its own phase lengths.

Worth keeping even though the answer was no: the **LUT format is decoded** (20 groups × 13 B =
260 B, 3-bit level selects, `TP` frame counts, ~20.0 ms frame period), the flash map is
`10 × (2080 + 220 + 200) = 25000 B`, and **no writable waveform RAM exists** (`0x93` RSRAM maps
only to image buffers — proved with an address-varying pattern).

**If a Spectra 6 panel with custom waveforms is ever wanted, choose by CONTROLLER not size:** the
3.7″ `GDEH037E01` uses an IST7163-family CoG, which is why Wenting Zhang's fast modes work there.

**Hardware safety recorded here:** a LUT fill of `0x01` is positive-only phases = **net DC bias**,
which can permanently damage e-paper. `RP` and `TP` share the fill byte, so a uniform fill can
never be both DC-balanced and short. **Never attempt OTP programming.**

**Process note, mine:** I spent a session re-deriving results already in
`host/results/spectra6/FINDINGS.md` and nearly hardware-tested an inference that file records as
already **failed** (temporal-plateau truncation → not a repeatable palette). The `SESSION_*` round
docs don't summarise FINDINGS.md. A validated **17-colour** palette already existed from
2026-10-31 — any new palette had to beat 17, not 6.

## Arc C — 16 colours on BWRY: found, then characterised (Nov 1 16:30 → Nov 2 early)

Transcripts `81b990e5`, `be3d9d44`. **→ `[[project_bwry_panel_rig]]`,
`[[project_bwry_16colour_characterisation]]`**

On the **2.9″ BWRY** (`EP29YR`, 168×384, GDEY029F51H glass, **JD79661**): register LUTs **work**,
unlike Spectra 6. 16 colours render via **3 register-waveform refreshes in ~26 s**, waveform slots

**Bug found and patched:** `yellow` and `red` are **swapped** in bb_epaper's table for this panel —
drawing `yellow` rendered red (L\*51.5) and vice versa, confirmed colorimetrically. The table
assigns hardware order `black,white,RED,YELLOW` while `BBEP_YELLOW==2`. Affects the generic
`rect`/`clear`/`bars` path only; `show16` has its own palette and was always right.

### Overnight characterisation, two units (Nov 1 20:55 → Nov 2 07:00)

- **Gamut peaks at 25 °C and falls off BOTH ways** — ≈−30% at 38 °C, also lower at 17 °C. Not
  monotonic, so a linear fit over the cold half predicts the wrong thing.
- **Unit-to-unit variation is large and real:** 14% gamut gap, dE00 6.40 ± 0.003 (~1855× the
  repeat noise). Worst cell burgundy, dE00 19.5.
- **No blue exists.** All 64 reachable triplets are neutral/orange/yellow/red; most negative b\* is
  −3.8. **Stop looking for blue on BWRY.**
- **A palette tuned on one unit can be 19% WORSE on another.** Always cross-validate.
- Shippable: burgundy `(3,0,1)` → `(2,1,2)` gives +12.8% gamut on one unit, −0.5% on the other,
  and raises chroma on both.

**Three methodology traps that produced confidently wrong answers:** an 8×8 scan grid is not a
proxy for full cell size (reads black at L\*74 vs L\*47; a palette optimised on it measured 23%
worse when rendered); **a triplet has no fixed colour** — neighbour crosstalk shifts it up to
10 L\*; and accept-if-better ratchets happily climb noise.

**Rig fact:** panel contrast is only **~6:1** and that is real, not glare.

## Arc D — Fleet survey: which panels do 16 colours (Nov 1 19:01 → Nov 3 12:53)

| Panel | Unit | Register LUT | 16 colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.9″ GDEY029F51H | ODBB1B76, ODBF2156 | live | ✅ reference |
| 4.2″ GDEM042F52 | ODCF0ED5 | live | ✅ |
| 7.5″ GDEM075F52 | ODBF064E | live **only post-init** | ✅ ~80 s |
| 3.5″ GDEM035F51 | OD507E22 | live, pre-PON | ✅ |
| 1.54″ GDEM0154F51H | OD1F9BE2 | live, pre-PON | ✅ |
| 2.6″ GDEY0266F51 | OD58FFC4 | live | ✅ |

**The 7.5″ taught the most important lesson of the arc.** I first reported it "not live" — true
only for the placement tested. Uploading the wave to `0x20` *before* PON (the order the 2.9″/4.2″
use) is **silently discarded**. Push it after the **entire** init including the `0xE0/0xE6/0xA5`
booster tail and the controller plays it. Proof is in durations: internal 20244 ms, colour wave
20909 ms, **edit wave 1862 ms** — an 11× change tracking which waveform was uploaded.

**So: a negative at one LUT placement says nothing about another placement.** The required control
is `wavec` vs `wavee` — two genuinely different waveforms, both with LUT_EN — **repeated at every
candidate placement**. A stock-vs-LUT A/B gave a confident false positive here because the PSR bit
produces a real, reproducible change by itself.

**Discriminate chroma from luma, never magnitude alone.** A different waveform shifts hue (channels
move by different amounts); ghosting, exposure drift and settling move all three equally. The 7.5″
showed a reproducible −7.3 luma at chroma ~1.1 — big enough to pass a magnitude threshold and
meaningless.

**Timeout trap:** the 4.2″ has unusable BUSY so `panels.yaml` gives it a **35 s fixed delay**, vs
the 2.9″'s 20637 ms. Two host bounds tuned on the 2.9″ silently aborted healthy uploads —
`render16.py`'s 30 s per-chunk ack (now 120 s) and the web tool's `PANEL_REFRESH_BUDGET_MS` (now
45 s). **A pass boundary can land on any chunk, so the wide bound must apply to every chunk.**

## Arc E — Rev 2: own driver, length-prefixed config (Nov 2 09:39 → Nov 3)

Transcripts `be3d9d44`, `d2f96336`, `8739a295`, `bc6ca52f`.
**→ `[[project_rev2_wire_fleet_state]]`, `[[project_silabs_config_size_ceiling]]`,
`[[project_bwry16_is_a_mode_not_a_scheme]]`, `[[project_palette_codes_are_panel_specific]]`**

The architectural arc. User's framing: move off bb_epaper to our own driver, **no framebuffer**, up
to 4 driving modes per display, and — stated explicitly and repeatedly — ***"I dont want hardcoded,
compiled in tables if possible, everything should live in the config."*** Plus temperature gating
per colour mode.

**The whole stack now speaks opendisplay-protocol 2.3 / structs 2.2.**

- Config ids **`0x80+` are length-prefixed** `[num][id][len:2 LE][body]` and **skippable** by a
  reader that doesn't know them.
- Drive packets **`0xAE`–`0xB1`**: mode (37 B head + sequence), LUT (4 B head + waveform), cal
  (4 B head + 8 B/entry), panel (13 B head + sleep).
- **`0x2E`–`0x31` are BURNED** — an earlier numbering. Parsers step over them by size and drop them
  on rewrite. Never reuse.
- `CMD_READ_IDENT` is **`0x0046`** (`0x0043` is FIRMWARE_VERSION — the review's suggested number
  collided).
- Sequence escapes `0x06` SET_PIN / `0x07` IMAGE_SLICE / `0x0B` LUT_SLICE implemented; unknown
  escape → NACK `0x07` SEQ_UNSUPPORTED **before any panel I/O**.

All five Silabs lab units OTA'd and configs rebuilt with
`Firmware_Silabs/panels/migrate_drive_config.py` (idempotent, preserves findmy `0x2D`).

### `0x0085 CMD_DRIVE_WRITE_START`

User pushed back on inventing it — *"cant we just run the pipeline write/normal write with
compression 3 times?"* The answer that held: the mode must be known **at START** because the
waveform uploads as part of init, before pixels. ACK is `[mode_used][passes][temp_c]`; the device
enforces temp_policy and **may SUBSTITUTE** a mode, so hosts must read the mode back. `temp_c` is
the EFR32 die sensor.

### Config size: the fight for space

**Ceiling is 4080 B** = `NVM3_DEFAULT_MAX_OBJECT_SIZE 4096` − 16 B record header, with the region
at 32768 (4 × 8192). **All three move together** — raising max-object alone cleared `nvm3_open`'s
threshold by **20 bytes** and failed in practice: config read back as 0 bytes, every write NACKed.
Data was not lost; reverting restored it.

**Growing the region is non-destructive** — `linker_nvm_end` is pinned to `__main_flash_end__` so
it grows *downward*. Verified on ODBB1B76: every object survived. The old "needs chip erase"
comment was wrong.

**4080 is a hard wall** regardless of region size. The lever that matters is **sharing waveform
slots across displays** — the firmware's `find_lut()` always matched on slot and ignored
`instance_number`, so slots were global; only the host duplicated them. Per-display: 3 displays →
4971 B (over). Shared: 5 displays → 3511 B.

**`od_cfg_index` has two traps.** It holds byte **offsets** and is built lazily only when empty, so
it must be rebuilt after any config write — stale offsets between two configs that both have drive
packets resolve to the **wrong bytes** rather than failing. And **`OD_CFG_INDEX_MAX` is 32 with
silent overflow**: the loop returns 32 like a success, and drive packets sort last, so a full index
drops exactly the drive data.

**Legacy-range packets must be INSERTED, never appended.** A TLV reader that doesn't know a
legacy (`<0x80`) type cannot know its length and must abandon the tail. py-opendisplay doesn't
model `0x2D findmy_config`, so appending drive packets after it on ODBF2156 put all 1560 B behind
that wall — the blob parsed back as *no drive data at all*.

**Notification loss, fixed:** `pipe_send_raw` dropped frames on `SL_STATUS_NO_MORE_RESOURCE`,
silently truncating chunked replies (a 2087 B config read returned 1223 B). Now retries on exactly
that status.

**The blob is no longer resident in RAM** on Firmware_Silabs — a 32×6 B index plus
`nvm3_readPartialData`. So that ceiling is **NVM3-bound, not RAM-bound**. Firmware_Unified is the
**opposite** (`od_config_asm.h` is a resident `uint8_t buffer[OD_CONFIG_MAX_SIZE]`, which is why
BG22 is capped at 2048 there). **Don't carry a claim across.**

### Colour scheme 9 vs mode 10 — a real mistake

Scheme **9** was added end-to-end (config.yaml, `epaper-dithering`, the designer UI mode selector,
py-opendisplay). Then: *"now the base 4 colour mode shows 16 gray colours instead of the 4."*

**`bwry16` is a MODE, not a colour scheme.** Scheme 10 belongs on the `0xAE` mode packet; writing
an unknown scheme into DisplayConfig `0x20` makes it **degrade to greyscale/mono silently**. `0x20`
stays BWRY(3).

**Palette codes are panel-specific:** 2.9″ code 2 = red, F52 code 2 = yellow. Permute before
writing `0xB0`, or yellow/red swap in all 16 entries.

### Web tool and host side

Mode selector in the designer UI; a debug test-swatch upload (forced raw, no dithering) for every
colour mode; `epaper-dithering` cloned and regenerated with the new palette. Bugs fixed along the
way: `DIRECT_WRITE_CHUNK_SIZE is not defined`, direct-write ack timeouts, QR code missing on boot.

**Review doc produced** at the user's request so others could review before go-live; the user
returned `driver-migration-review.md` and `config-length-prefix-proposal.md` with external
feedback, which fed the Nov 3 implementation.

### Still open from this arc

**`[[project_bwry16_palette_yellow_red_unresolved]]`** — the nominal palette (`code_map [0,1,3,2]`,
entry 2 = red) and the photo measurement (entry 2 = strong **yellow**, L\*92.9 b\*64.9) **disagree
and neither is verified**. The photo can't arbitrate because `measure_photo.py` white-balances each
panel against its own white entry, so the one cell that could have fixed grid orientation was

full-screen flat bands of panel codes 0/1/2/3, photograph. Not yet run. If the photo order is
wrong, yellow and red are transposed in every 16-colour upload.

## Arc F — Unknown 2.6″ BW panel: solved (Nov 2 19:47 → Nov 3 09:00)

Transcript `bc6ca52f`. **→ `[[project_newton26_bw_panel_driving]]`, `[[project_ee04_uart_lab_board]]`**

An unlabelled BW panel went in the chamber: *"try to find out what ic it is."* Ran under a
session-scoped Stop hook overnight. **Solved** — text, shapes and real ArUco markers render and
**decode back off the camera**.

Panel: **"newton PRO 2.6in BW Freezer", EL026F6W4A**. The `IC:` on the label is an Industry Canada
number, not the controller.

```
pwr on; reset
w 04                  PON   -> BUSY low ~100-260 ms, then high
w 00 1F 0B            PSR
w 61 01 18 00 78      TRES  (280 x 120)
w 50 97               CDI
<8400 bytes to 0x10>  DTM1
w 92                  PTOU  (whole panel, not last window)
w 12                  DRF   -> BUSY releases in ~2475 ms
```

**The mapping is COLUMN-major, which is what made this hard:** byte *k* → column `k // 25`, offset
`k % 25`. 25 bytes = 200 px per column, 8400 B = 336 columns, 1 bpp, MSB topmost, bit 1 = white,
**column order mirrored**. Settled by splitting every column black-top/white-bottom and sweeping
bytes-per-column: at N=25 the boundary is a clean horizontal line; 15/20/24/30 all shear into
vertical stripes.

**Every earlier failure assumed ROW-major.** That produced stripes and phantom "4× repeats" and
sent me chasing 2bpp, 4bpp and a dozen geometries. **If structured content stripes but uniform
fills are clean, suspect the major order before the pixel format.**

Other traps: **`TRES 120x280` is REJECTED while `280x120` is accepted** — same byte count, same
data, and the rejection looks exactly like a wedged panel (BUSY still cycles a normal 2475 ms). I
wrongly reported the panel as damaged and needing physical intervention. A half/half test **cannot
distinguish 1bpp from 2bpp** and I briefly "proved" 2bpp with one. And the panel **physically
moved** in the chamber, drifting the ROI off the glass and silently flipping apparent polarity.

**The lab board has no read channel, and the obvious test for one lies.** Reading the data pin back
returns stable, plausible bytes. Pull-up vs pull-down does **not** settle it — the internal pulls
never dominate. **The control that works is reading with the panel UNPOWERED**: identical bytes,
proving it's the host pin's residual state. Also `refresh` printing `temp=08` is a **hardcoded
constant** (`fastTempE6`), not a reading — and it tells the controller it's 8 °C, selecting a cold
waveform.

**Only one of the chamber's two cameras sees the screen** — `hq` looks at the *back* of the tag
(its FCC label); the wide-angle `usb` sees the front. I wrongly concluded "no visual verification
is possible" from the `hq` view alone.

## Arc G — Faster refresh on the 2.6″ (Nov 3 13:50 → Nov 4 20:00)

Transcript `bc6ca52f`. **→ `[[project_ep266_fast_and_partial]]`**

User's priority, stated explicitly: ***"I am looking for a fast 4 colour update, fast 16 is nice but
the primary goal is a fast BWRY or BW update."***

**The PLL register `0x30` is the real lever — 1.88×, essentially free.** Stock init hardcodes
`30 08`; the low nibble is a frame-rate code and **`0x07` is the fastest safe value: 19433 →
10341 ms**. Confirmed on the shipped `panels.yaml` sequence, not just bb_epaper's.

```
0x00,0x01 hang | 0x02 24567 | 0x03 18953 | 0x04 16390 | 0x05 14477
0x06 12386 | 0x07 10341 | 0x08 19436 (stock) | 0x0C-0x0E ~19400 | 0x10 hang
```

**Colour cost, corrected:** dE **5–6**, not the "dE 3, essentially free" an earlier bb_epaper-init
measurement suggested — red C\* −21%, and **white picks up chroma 5 → 10**, the most visible part.
Good trade for 1.88×, but not free and should not be sold as such.

**The booster tail is a separate −27%** (19392 → 14244 ms) via `E0 02 / E6 xx / A5` before PON.
**The E6 value is irrelevant** — 0x40/0x50/0x68/0x7F all land on ~14245 ms. It is binary, not a
knob; I wasted a session sweeping it. **All three registers are required** — `E0 02 + A5` without
E6 wedges the panel. Note `A5` here takes **no argument** and sits **before** PON, while the
1.54″/3.5″ fast inits use `A5 00` **after** PON. Same registers, different arity and placement —
do not merge them.

**Real temperature is a big lever; forced temperature is not.** At 31.8 °C: PLL 0x08 → 14006 ms,
PLL 0x07 → 8551 ms. The panel reads its **own** sensor and picks a shorter waveform when warm; the
`E0/E6/A5` "forced temperature" path does not. **"E6 is inert" does not imply "temperature is
inert"** — I made exactly that inference and only actual heat disproved it. Consequence: **any
fixed delay or BUSY timeout must be sized for the COLD case.**

**Ghosting is binary** — reset phase or not; truncation doesn't shorten it. **Windowed partial
refresh: dead** on this part. LUT-driven BW reaches 1.8 s but ghosts.

## Recurring methodology lessons

Collected because each one cost real hardware time more than once.

1. **A negative result is scoped to its exact configuration.** The 7.5″ LUT was written off on one
   placement; "E6 is inert" was generalised to "temperature is inert". Both wrong, both took heat
   or a re-test to disprove.
2. **Have a control that can fail.** The 2×2 closed Spectra 6; stock-vs-LUT A/B gave a false
   positive. Reading a pin with the panel **unpowered** is the only control that detects a phantom
   read channel.
3. **A plausible-looking reading is often the measuring apparatus.** `temp=08` was a constant,
   43 A was the wrong serial port, the pin read was the host's own residual state.
4. **Separate "it changed" from "it changed the way I claim".** Chroma vs luma; per-band vs
   whole-panel mean.
5. **Read the project's own prior findings first.** FINDINGS.md, `docs/TOOLCHAINS.md`,
   `simple-config-presets.json`. Three separate days lost to re-deriving or re-searching something
   already written down.
6. **Camera the panel.** A textbook current trace or a clean BUSY duration can accompany a garbage
   render — and the ROI silently lies after anything moves.
7. **Silent-success failure modes are everywhere in this stack:** I²C ACKs non-existent registers,
   `od_cfg_find` serves the first duplicate, `od_cfg_index` returns 32 as success, unknown colour
   schemes degrade to greyscale, unknown legacy config ids abandon the tail, dropped BLE
   notifications truncate replies. Prefer a loud refusal to a plausible answer.
